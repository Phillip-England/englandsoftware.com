## Why the original model needs a narrower boundary

The original `business/PRICING.md` offered a **$300 flat** home page plus an unspecified number of service pages, a built contact form, and included email setup. It proposed **$10/month** for form/email handling without defining whether that meant delivery, notifications, or a mailbox. Basic hosting was **$20/month or $200/year** without a precise maintenance boundary. Custom applications were quoted after a free consultation, with **$500** as an internal starting thought for a very small app. These are recorded here for comparison; the table above is the proposed public offer.

At $300, 6 total project hours yield $50/hour before operating costs and taxes. At 10 hours it yields $30/hour; at 15 hours, $20/hour. Those are gross receipts, not take-home pay. **Provisional internal rule:** quote larger work from estimated total hours at no less than $50/hour plus direct project costs, then check whether that covers nonbillable time and desired income. Keep the $300 package only if it can usually finish within 6 total hours. Track **all** discovery, content chasing, design, build, revision, launch, and immediate support time on the first three projects. If it takes longer, raise the price or narrow the package. The $500 application thought is too low to act as a floor without a time estimate; at 20 hours it is only $25/hour before costs. Use an estimate based on work and responsibility for every application.

Annual hosting at $200 is effectively $16.67 per month. As an illustration, if direct hosting and payment costs were $5 per client per month, only $11.67 per month would remain to cover support, administration, and profit. One unexpected support request can erase that margin. Track actual per-client infrastructure, payment, monitoring, support, and billing time before committing to a large hosting book. Review any existing site before accepting it at the basic rate; migration or unusual maintenance needs a separate quote.

## Decisions to make before accepting clients

- Choose a payment schedule and put the $80/hour rate and case-by-case scope in every proposal involving later work.
- Define response times, cancellation/renewal terms, and backup responsibility for hosting.
- Specify and test the working form's email delivery method and spam controls for each build. A mailbox and form delivery are different products.
- Review the first three projects' actual hours and hosting costs, then revise the prices.
